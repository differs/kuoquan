# Epilogue · Identity and Choice

This book began with a question:

> "If I can keep my position and extend my life by sacrificing part of the people, should I do it?"

Now we have reached the place where a complete answer can be given.

## 1. The Circle Is a Choice, Not an Identity

One word recurs throughout this book: the circle. And the most important thing about the circle is this:

> **The circle is not something you are; it is something you do.**

"We" was never a given. It is drawn by **one concrete decision after another**:

- Whom do you count as a person?
- Whom do you give a stake?
- Over how long a time span do you make decisions?
- When interest tempts you to tighten the circle, do you tighten it?

**Each of these four questions is a choice; not one is decided by bloodline.**

This is why the "superior race" is a false concept. **No group is born inside the circle.** The circle's boundary is drawn by action — count in one more person today, and the circle grows a little; exclude one more today, and it shrinks a little.

## 2. To Those Who Hold Power

If you hold power — even if only over a department, a company, a family — this book gives you a checklist:

1. **How many people does this institution count as "us"?**
2. **Do I rule by fear, or by consent?**
3. **Over what time scale do I make decisions?**
4. **Are the people I cut away the very resource I will most need in the future?**

You need not believe human nature is good. You need only see the arithmetic clearly:

> **Extraction is the short-term shortcut; inclusion is the long-term compound interest.**

An order that needs only obedience gets the obedient;
an order that gives people a stake gets defenders.

**The difference is not in moral loftiness, but in length of time.**

## 3. To Those Drawn Outside the Circle

If you hold no power, this book gives you a mirror:

- See clearly which circle you are in, and by whom you were drawn outside;
- see clearly what keeps this order going, and how much longer it can hold;
- **and most important — see that your silence is itself a form of influence.** For the ultimate source of legitimacy is the tacit consent of the governed.

You need not wait to be included. **You can draw your own circle first.**

What can one person do? — Turn the small patch you can influence into a place with credibility, with reciprocity, that treats people as people. **A small circle is the seed of a large one.** Every larger "we" in history began with someone deciding to widen the circle a little.

## 4. To Everyone

What this book asks is not kindness. Kindness is cheap, and unreliable.

It asks for **clarity**:

- to know clearly that the circle can be drawn;
- to know clearly that every order is betting on how long it can live;
- to know clearly that **the temptation of the narrow circle never goes away, because in the short run it is always the better deal.**

Then, before every temptation, make the choice that **conforms to the long-term arithmetic.**

## Last

Back to the first question — "should we sacrifice part of the people."

The answer should now be clear:

**It is not a moral question. It is a question of time.**

Those who see only today will always choose sacrifice;
those who see three generations will not;
those who see civilization will bring more people into the circle.

**The span of time you take responsibility for is exactly the size of circle you can see.**

And civilization, in the end, is nothing but **people stretching the time they take responsibility for, again and again** — from only the self, to the family, to the nation, to all humanity, until one day, to a future not yet arrived.

> **Expanding the circle is giving yourself back to time.**

**That is the whole secret.**
